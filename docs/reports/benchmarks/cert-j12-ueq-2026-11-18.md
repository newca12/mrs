# CASC-J12 UEQ campaign (2026-11-18)

> This separate UEQ campaign returned **144/300 raw `Unsatisfiable` answers**.
> Its bundled strict audit reports **144 `VerifiedGood`, 0 `VerifiedBad`, and 0
> `Unknown`**. One additional row, `LAT183-10`, ended as `ResourceOut` at the
> host's 54,198 MB effective-memory ceiling. This is a single observation from
> a dirty producer tree on a 16-physical-core host with 54,198 MB effective
> memory, not a canonical CASC score or a matched comparison. The strict audit
> used the recorded TPTP v10.0.0 path, whose library tree is not hash-bound; it
> is not an exact J12 v9.0.0 re-audit.

## Search results

The campaign completed all 300 UEQ rows. Every definitive answer matched the
reference status; all 144 refutations have `verdict=ok` in `run.csv`.

All 300 processes exited with code 0 and none has a recorded termination
signal. The seven `GaveUp` rows are `CSR036-10`, `CSR038-10`, `CSR049-10`,
`CSR058-10`, `CSR065-10`, `LAT391-1`, and `LAT392-1`. Their search telemetry
reports `saturated=0`; they should not be described as saturated searches.

The official J12 UEQ budget applied here was **180 seconds**. Although the
metadata's `time_limit`/`default_time_limit` fields say 120, `use_casc_times=1`
and `run.log` confirms `ueq=180s`; the per-row timeouts are consistent with that
budget.

The `mrs-starexec` adapter auto-selected `casc_ueq`, with eight workers and
strategy IDs `[4, 8, 12, 11, 2, 14, 15, 1]`. Cross-strategy equality sharing was
disabled (`MRS_SHARED_POOL_INTERVAL=0`), and the campaign ran one problem at a
time (`jobs=1`). This is an observation of that portfolio and build, not a
strategy-selection experiment.

## Comparison with the November 17 UEQ campaign

The November 17 campaign returned 147 refutations; its subsequent exact
v9.0.0 strict re-audit verified all 147. The November 18 run returned 144,
with all 144 verified by its bundled strict audit under the recorded v10.0.0
environment. Their solve sets share **143 problems**: four were solved only on
November 17, and one only on November 18. Six rows have different final
statuses:

| Problem | 2026-11-17 | 2026-11-18 |
|:---|:---|:---|
| `BOO023-1` | `Unsatisfiable` (145.525 s) | `Timeout` (176.064 s) |
| `BOO073-1` | `Unsatisfiable` (80.329 s) | `Timeout` (176.493 s) |
| `GRP667-10` | `Unsatisfiable` (91.610 s) | `Timeout` (176.455 s) |
| `LCL416-10` | `Unsatisfiable` (62.234 s) | `Timeout` (175.283 s) |
| `LAT085-1` | `GaveUp` (175.251 s) | `Unsatisfiable` (175.098 s) |
| `LAT183-10` | `Timeout` (177.725 s) | `ResourceOut` (179.819 s) |

Both runs used the same eight-worker `casc_ueq` order, disabled sharing,
`jobs=1`, and the 180-second J12 UEQ limit. They are nevertheless not a
controlled comparison: they used different dirty producer builds and hosts.
The November 18 host had only 54,198 MB effective memory, versus 108,816 MB on
the November 17 host; neither host provided the configured 131,072 MB allowance,
and `casc` does not pin the workers to eight physical cores. The solve-set
changes therefore do not establish a schedule or source effect, and the
one-run difference should not be attributed to portfolio nondeterminism alone.

On the 143 common solves, the sum of reported wall times is 3,345.827 s for
November 18 versus 3,620.250 s for November 17 (means 23.397 s and 25.316 s).
This is a success-conditioned, cross-host descriptive statistic, not a matched
speedup measurement.
