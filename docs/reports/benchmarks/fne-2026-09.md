# FNE: where the coverage actually goes, and one dead portfolio worker

> Status: current. Branch `feat/fne-casc-sim-exploration`, commit `ebb99f6`.
> Measured on `crates/mrs-bench/problems/casc-30` and `cert-30-20260926` /
> `cert-j13-20260926`. Development host: 2 physical cores / 16 GB
> (`docs/results/perf/bank.tsv`, `intel-r-core-tm-i3-5010u-cpu-x2`), so every
> number below is either a banked CASC-30 measurement or is explicitly flagged
> as a *pessimistic* local proxy — this host cannot represent a CASC entry and
> `casc-sim` says so in its own warnings.

## 1. The FNE score and where it leaks

| edition | FNE solved | of |
|---|---|---|
| CASC-30 (`cert-30-20260926`) | 43 | 100 |
| CASC-J13 (`cert-j13-20260926`) | 35 | 100 |

The 57 CASC-30 failures are not spread evenly. By TPTP domain:

| family | failures | of | note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **LCL** | **30** | 37 | propositional theoremhood (`is_a_theorem`) |
| KRS | 6 | 10 | ordinary hard FOL |
| NUN | 6 | 9 | ordinary hard FOL |
| NLP | 3 | 3 | hit a resource ceiling, see §3 |
| CSR | 2 | 24 | ordinary hard FOL |
| GEO, SWB, SYN, COM, BOO, SWV, SYO | 9 | 12 | ordinary hard FOL |

**One domain is 30 of the 57 failures.** LCL is not a search-power problem in
the ordinary sense: 30 of the 37 LCL problems are three-to-five FOF formulas of
under 600 bytes. `LCL062+1` is 545 bytes and `LCL978+1` is 420 bytes, and CASC
solves both. What mrs does to them is this:

```
LCL978+1  s1  Timeout  processed=522 generated=14811  (30 s, 1 worker)
```

Eight portfolio strategies, each given the full 240 s, together about 42 000
given-clause iterations on a **three-clause** problem, and no refutation. The
clause set is 100 % Horn with 2 positive units; the whole problem is `p(s)` for
a schema `s`, the detachment rule `p(X) ∧ p(X→Y) → p(Y)`, and the goal. A
refutation is a five-step *condensed detachment* derivation, and finding one is
a search over that derivation shape, not a saturation over a Herbrand universe.

## 2. Two LCL sub-clusters, two different problems

| sub-cluster | problems | unsolved | what it needs |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A — condensed detachment schemata | 26 | 24 | a propositional-logic proof search |
| B — big ground propositional | 11 | 6 | a CDCL solver |

Sub-cluster A is 3–6 tiny FOF formulas. The axioms are *schemata* — a
universally quantified propositional formula inside the predicate — plus the
condensed-detachment rule, and the conjecture is a schematic propositional
formula (Tarski's condensed positive forms, `cn_n`). Sub-cluster B has an
8 KB–385 KB ground conjecture and is ordinary propositional SAT;
`LCL642+1.015` (39 KB) is already solved.

Sub-cluster A is the interesting one, and 19 of its 24 failures have a
**classically valid** target. The relevant theory — `cn_1` (transitivity),
`cn_2` (`(¬X→X)→X`), `cn_3` (`X→(¬X→Y)`) and modus ponens — generates only
propositional tautologies, so a tautological target is *necessary* for
derivability and mrs's `GaveUp` is a search failure, not a wrong answer. The
7 remaining sub-cluster A problems (all with the large `f2` schema) have
**non**-tautological targets and are a genuinely different problem.

I did not solve sub-cluster A, and the reason is worth recording so the next
attempt starts from the right place. The natural formulation is a
goal-directed search on

```
deriv(F) = fact(F)                                  F is an instance of a schema
         | exists k, P_1..P_k : spine = P_k→(…→(P_1→F)) is a fact
                                   and deriv(P_1) … and deriv(P_k)
```

Every modus-ponens chain ends at a fact, so this covers the whole derivation
read backwards, and matching the spine by unification bounds the branching
factor by `|schemas| × depth`. It was prototyped end to end. It does not close
the problems, and the failure is instructive rather than a bug: the formulation
forces the deepest link of every chain to be a *fact*, so it cannot use a
derived clause that serves several instantiations at once — which is exactly
what the mrs proofs of `LCL982+1` and `LCL390+1` do. A bounded forward
implication closure (Tarski's own method) does not close them either: the pool
has to grow past the goal's letters before anything fires, and at
`size_bound=60` it has 8 104 propositions and still misses `LCL062+1`.

So sub-cluster A needs a real propositional-logic engine — tabled
goal-directed resolution over the finite Horn program, with term creation, or
a semantic (BDD) method. That is a research-scale piece of work, not a
strategy change, and it is worth 30 CASC problems. It is the single largest
FNE lever and it is the one thing on this list that cannot be tuned into
existence.

## 3. casc-sim: these are not memory problems

`casc-sim` was used to separate "would have timed out at CASC" from "got past
the CASC limit and then hit a resource ceiling", on the FNE failures this host
can actually supply memory for (banked peak ≤ ~11 GB, so the 128 GiB
casc-sim allowance is not an OOM trap on a 16 GB box):

| problem | banked 240 s | casc-sim 480 s | iterations at the CASC limit | iterations at 480 s |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| LCL656+1.015 | Timeout, 2.1 GB | Timeout | 219 | 2 707 |
| SYN986+1.004 | GaveUp, 2.1 GB | GaveUp | 128 | 597 |
| COM008+1 | GaveUp, 6.2 GB | GaveUp | 13 927 | 112 666 |
| LCL682+1.020 | Timeout, 6.2 GB | Timeout | 23 365 | 55 930 |

Every one reports `casc_limit_passed_ms≈240000` — the run really did get past
the CASC limit — and every one is in the same place when it does. Two of them
(SYN986+1.004, LCL656+1.015) do fewer than 3 000 given-clause iterations in
eight minutes on a *three-clause* problem. Nothing crossed a resource ceiling
except LCL682+1.020, which spent 173 622 of 403 905 generated clauses on LRS
pruning. And every run reports `passive:0` in `state_at_casc_limit`: the passive
queue has been emptied by the LRS prune at the moment the CASC limit passes, so
the search is thrashing rather than accumulating.

The banked peaks are 2–11 GB against a 128 GiB allowance, and the banked
`telemetry` shows `lrs_discarded` at 80–90 % of `generated` with
`weight_discarded=0`. The failure mode is arithmetic, not allocation: the
search spends its budget generating clauses and discarding them. The one
exception is `NLP262+1`, the single `ResourceOut` in the division, which
reached 62 GB — that one *is* memory-bound and is a different problem from the
other 56.

Because the failures are compute-bound, casc-sim cannot improve the CASC score
by itself: CASC stops the run at the 240 s limit regardless. Its value here is
diagnostic — it rules memory out and points the search at throughput and
redundancy elimination.

## 4. Two portfolio workers were dead

`casc_fne` rewrites every slot's `AllNegative` literal selection to the
single-negative `MaxNegative` (AGENTS.md §7 calls this "single-negative
literal selection avoids the all-negative resolution blow-up"). That interacts
badly with a Horn rule clause:

```
rule:  ~p(X) | p(Y) | ~p(X => Y)      AllNegative -> {~p(X), ~p(X=>Y)}
                                        MaxNegative -> {~p(X=>Y)} only
```

A rule clause with both negative and positive literals can then never supply
the *selected* positive literal that a lone negative goal unit needs. On the
LCL encodings the goal is exactly such a unit, and the only clause
`~p(X=>Y)` unifies with is the axiom schema — both at the axiom distance. With
`set-of-support` also enabled (s10, s13), the gate requires one parent with
`distance < sos_depth`, so that inference is forbidden too. Result:

```
LCL978+1  s10  GaveUp  processed=3 generated=0  elapsed_ms=71
LCL978+1  s13  GaveUp  processed=3 generated=0
```

Two of the eight FNE workers generate nothing at all, on any of the 30 LCL
failures, for the whole 238 s. They are not searching badly; they are not
searching. `MRS_MAX_TERM_WEIGHT` sweeps at 60/150/400/1200 change nothing
here (the derived clauses are small — peak RSS in a 30 s single-worker run is
1.2 GB), which rules out clause size as the lever and is why the fix targets
the restriction instead.

`fix(sos)` in this branch adds a pre-flight over the input clauses: release
the restriction when, and only when, at least one input pair is
inference-capable **and** every such pair has both parents outside the support
set. After it, on the same problem and the same budget:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| s10 on `LCL978+1` | `GaveUp processed=3 generated=0` (71 ms) | `Timeout processed=376 generated=8614` |
| s13 on `LCL978+1` | `GaveUp processed=3 generated=0` | `Timeout processed=443 generated=11907` |
| `casc_fne` on `LCL062+1`, 40 s | 1 508 clauses processed (extrapolated from the banked 8 973 in 238 s) | 3 467 processed, 91 760 generated |

Both tests cover the decision in both directions on the real `LCL978+1` clause
shape. Because releasing the restriction can only add inferences — and added
inferences can in principle change which clauses LRS keeps — the fix was also
A/B'd against a binary built from `main`, on the 13 fastest banked-solved
problems across four divisions at a 40 s budget:

```
feq  ALG210+2 MGT061+1 SWV408+2 GEO524+1     before == after  (Theorem ×4)
fne  LCL982+1 NUN081+1 NUN060+1 MGT067+1      before == after  (Theorem ×4)
ueq  COL002-5 PUZ131-10 REL008-3 CSR051-10    before == after  (Unsatisfiable ×4)
icu  EEE001+1                                 before == after  (Theorem)
```

13/13 identical: the pre-flight is silent on problems where SOS is working.

**Honest scoreboard: this fixes a real defect and buys ~2.3x throughput on the
LCL cluster, and it changes the FNE score by 0.** The refutations are not
reachable by any amount of extra throughput — see §2. It is also not
LCL-specific in the sense that matters: any problem whose input admits only
axiom-side inferences was silently getting a dead worker, and the pre-flight
now finds those everywhere, not just here.

## 5. What I would do next, in order

1. **Sub-cluster A: a propositional-logic engine.** 30 problems, +30 FNE. Needs
   tabled goal-directed resolution over the Horn program with term creation, or
   a BDD/semantic method, plus a TSTP proof path. A `condensed_detachment`
   pre-pass alongside `fvo` in `run_schedule` is the natural home, and the
   fail-closed discipline that `fvo` already uses is the right shape: a
   pre-pass that can only add a refutation, verified by building the derivation
   out of real resolutions and requiring the final clause to be the empty one.
2. **Sub-cluster B: extend the FVO pre-pass to ground propositional input.**
   For a ground clause set, "atom → propositional variable indexed by the
   atom" is exact rather than an abstraction, CaDiCaL is already vendored, and
   a resolution proof lifts verbatim. 6 problems.
3. **Re-fit `casc_fne` after (1) and (2).** The current order was fitted on
   30 s solo sweeps whose union coverage is 42/42 — i.e. every problem that any
   strategy solves at 30 s. It says nothing about the 240 s budget or about
   the cluster that dominates the failures.

## Reproducing

```bash
# failure profile
python3 - <<'EOF'
import csv, collections
rows = list(csv.DictReader(open(
    'crates/mrs-bench/results/cert-30-20260926/run.csv')))
fne = [r for r in rows if r['division'] == 'fne']
fam = lambda p: p.rstrip('0123456789.+').rstrip('+')
bad = collections.Counter(fam(r['problem']) for r in fne
                          if r['szs_status'] != 'Theorem')
print(sorted(bad.items(), key=lambda kv: -kv[1]))
EOF

# the dead worker, before/after (this branch is "after")
for s in 10 13; do
  ./target/release/mrs --time 10 --workers 1 --strategy $s \
    crates/mrs-bench/problems/casc-30/FNE/LCL978+1.p 2>&1 | grep 'SZS detail'
done

# casc-sim characterisation
./target/release/mrs --hardware casc-sim --sim-time-factor 2 \
  --schedule casc_fne --time 240 \
  crates/mrs-bench/problems/casc-30/FNE/LCL978+1.p
```
